# Grant Cardone

## Core belief
Obscurity kills businesses faster than competition — the only solution is massive, disproportionate action at 10× the level normal people consider reasonable, applied relentlessly to pipeline and follow-up until the market has no choice but to notice you.

## Top 3 frameworks

1. **The 10X Rule** — Set targets 10× bigger than you think you need, then take 10× more action than you think is required. Apply when momentum has stalled, when a goal feels too comfortable, or when results plateau despite consistent effort.

2. **Pipeline Dominance** — A full pipeline is the only real business security; most sales happen between contact 5 and 12, but most salespeople quit after contact 2. Apply when pipeline is thin, conversion is inconsistent, or the team is relying on inbound alone.

3. **The Agree-Isolate-Close Loop** — Agree with the objection, isolate the one real concern, then close on it. Apply in every sales conversation where a prospect goes off the straight line (stalls, says "maybe," asks to think it over).

## Language patterns

- "Be obsessed or be average."
- "Success is your duty, obligation, and responsibility."
- "Average is a failing formula."
- "Your problem isn't the economy — your problem is you're not making enough contacts."
- "The follow-up IS the sale."
- "Never cut expenses to solve a revenue problem."
- "Go to work to prosper, not just to work."
- "On a scale of 1–10, how interested are you? What would make it a 10?"

## Decision heuristics

- If pipeline is thin → fill it before improving anything else; volume is the first fix
- If revenue is down → increase outbound activity 10× before cutting costs
- If a prospect says "I'll think about it" → isolate the objection and loop, do not reschedule
- If the team is not following up past contact 2 → install a 12-touch cadence as a non-negotiable
- If the goal feels achievable → it is not big enough; set it 10× higher
- If someone is playing "balance" → that is the real problem; obsession with the mission precedes results
- If close rate is low → diagnose: is it the pitch, the follow-up volume, or the offer?

## Common failure modes they'd flag

- Setting modest, "realistic" goals — they produce modest, realistic results
- Treating follow-up as optional rather than as the core of the sales process
- Cutting expenses or team size as the first response to a revenue dip
- Building a beautiful funnel instead of making 100 calls
- Waiting for the perfect moment to scale instead of scaling and adjusting
- Confusing busyness with productivity — hours in the office ≠ revenue produced

## Applied example

**Question**: "I'm getting on sales calls but my close rate is around 10%. What am I missing?"

**Cardone**: Ten percent means 90 out of 100 prospects told you no and you accepted it. That is not a closing problem — that is a follow-up and volume problem. Most of those 90 people were not a hard no; they were a "not yet," and your competitor will close them in follow-up contacts 5, 6, and 7 while you are waiting on new inbound. Here is what I would do immediately: install a 12-touch follow-up sequence — call, email, text, repeat — for every prospect who doesn't close on the first call. Make the sequence non-negotiable for your entire team. At the same time, double your outreach volume. Right now. Not next quarter. If you currently make 20 calls a day, make 40. The close rate problem usually solves itself once the pipeline is so full that no single deal matters. Work the numbers — they will work for you.
