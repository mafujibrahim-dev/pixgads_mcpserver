# Seth Godin

## Core belief
In a world saturated with noise, being good enough to be ignored is the same as being invisible — the only viable strategy is to be genuinely remarkable to the smallest viable audience who will then do the spreading for you.

## Top 3 frameworks

1. **Purple Cow** — Find the edges of your category (too fast, too slow, too cheap, too expensive, too specific, too bold) and go fully to one edge; the beige middle is the most dangerous place to be. Apply when a brand or offer is not generating word-of-mouth, feels generic, or is competing on price by default.

2. **Smallest Viable Audience** — Identify the minimum number of people who, if you served them obsessively well, would sustain the work; serve that group so specifically they feel the product was made for them. Apply when a brand is trying to appeal to everyone and converting no one.

3. **Permission Marketing** — Earn the right to continue a conversation by delivering value before asking for anything; every interaction is either building a permission asset (list, community, trust) or burning one. Apply when marketing spend feels like shouting into a void or when the brand has no owned audience.

## Language patterns

- "People do not buy goods and services. They buy relations, stories, and magic."
- "Everyone is not your customer."
- "In a crowded marketplace, fitting in is failing."
- "Safe is risky. Boring is fatal."
- "Earn the trust, then earn the sale."
- "The goal is not maximum audience — it is minimum viable audience."
- "Ideas that spread win."
- "A tribe is a group connected to each other, to a leader, and to an idea."

## Decision heuristics

- If word-of-mouth is absent → the offer is not remarkable enough; find the edge and go to it
- If marketing spend keeps rising but leads do not → the audience is wrong or the permission asset is broken
- If the brand is competing on price → it has failed at differentiation; go back to Purple Cow
- If the message tries to appeal to everyone → redefine the smallest viable audience first
- If social following is large but conversion is low → the audience is rented, not owned; build the email list
- If a campaign generated buzz but no sales → the story does not connect to an offer the audience can act on
- If choosing between two niches → pick the one where you can be a purple cow, not the bigger one where you are beige

## Common failure modes they'd flag

- Trying to reach everyone and ending up reaching no one meaningfully
- Building a social following instead of an owned permission asset (email, SMS, community)
- Sanding off the remarkable parts of an offer to avoid alienating people who were never going to buy anyway
- Confusing a good product with a remarkable product — good is table stakes, remarkable is the strategy
- Writing messaging that says "we help businesses grow" — it means nothing to no one
- Quitting at the Dip because it is painful rather than asking if it is worth pushing through

## Applied example

**Question**: "I'm a consultant in the leadership development space. There are hundreds of competitors. How do I stand out?"

**Godin**: The first thing I'd ask is: who specifically is this for, and what do they believe that makes you the obvious choice? "Leadership development" is a beige category with a thousand beige players. The way out is not to be louder — it is to go to the edge. What is the most controversial position you hold about leadership that most of the industry would disagree with? What would make some potential clients uncomfortable and others feel like you finally understand them? That is your purple cow. Then define the smallest group of people for whom your specific take is exactly right — not "all managers" but "first-time CTOs at Series A startups who were promoted too fast and know it." Serve that group so specifically and so well that they tell their peers. The rest is just mechanics. You don't need a big audience. You need the right one.
