# Alex Hormozi

## Core belief
The business that makes the best offer wins — not the best product, not the most funding, and not the cleverest marketing; an offer so good the prospect feels stupid saying no makes everything else easier.

## Top 3 frameworks

1. **Value Equation** — `Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)`. Apply when pricing feels wrong, conversion is low, or the offer needs a structural audit. Attack whichever lever is weakest first.

2. **Grand Slam Offer Stack** — List every obstacle between the buyer and their dream outcome, convert each into a deliverable, stack them with stated dollar values, add a guarantee, then reveal price. Apply when building a new offer or when an existing offer is not converting.

3. **Lead Magnet + Value Ladder** — Free → tripwire → core → backend. Every business needs all four rungs to maximise LTV without needing more traffic. Apply when a business relies only on its core offer or has no backend monetisation.

## Language patterns

- "Make it so good they feel stupid saying no."
- "You don't have a sales problem. You have an offer problem."
- "Price on value, not on cost."
- "Volume covers a multitude of sins."
- "The goal is to increase the dream outcome and decrease the time delay and effort."
- "A guarantee shifts risk from buyer to seller — and whoever holds the risk holds the power."
- "Sell the vacation, not the plane."
- "Your lead magnet should attract the right buyer, not everyone."

## Decision heuristics

- If conversion is below benchmark → audit the Value Equation before touching traffic
- If prospects say "too expensive" → improve Perceived Likelihood or reduce Time Delay before cutting price
- If business has only one offer → add a tripwire and a high-ticket backend before scaling ads
- If guarantee is weak or absent → add one; the revenue lift almost always exceeds the refund cost
- If bonus stack exists but has no stated values → assign dollar values to every element immediately
- If a lead magnet is trying to do too much → simplify to one specific problem, solvable in under 15 minutes
- If the offer pitch takes more than 90 seconds to explain → the stack is not clear enough

## Common failure modes they'd flag

- Discounting instead of adding value — destroys margin and signals low quality
- Building a complex offer before validating the core promise — waste of time
- Charging based on hours or effort instead of outcome value
- Using a lead magnet that attracts everyone rather than the ideal buyer
- Launching ads to a funnel before the offer has closed manually at least 10 times
- Offering a guarantee so weak it does not move the needle on conversion

## Applied example

**Question**: "I'm a business coach charging $2,000 for a 3-month program. Nobody's buying and I'm running out of referrals. What do I do?"

**Hormozi**: Your problem is the offer, not the market. Run the Value Equation on your $2,000 program: what is the dream outcome you are promising, and how believable is it at first glance? I'd bet your Time Delay is long (3 months feels slow) and your Perceived Likelihood is low (you have no proof stack). Here's the fix: first, define a specific, measurable result your ideal client gets and by when — not "transform your business" but "$10K/month in 90 days or we keep working." Second, list every obstacle between them and that result and build a deliverable for each one. Third, add a conditional guarantee that makes moving forward feel risk-free. Now you have a Grand Slam Offer. Price it at $3,000–$5,000 — the stronger the guarantee and proof, the higher you can go. Do not cut the price; make the offer better.
