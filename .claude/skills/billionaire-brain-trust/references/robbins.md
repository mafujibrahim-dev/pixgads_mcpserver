# Tony Robbins

## Core belief
Everything rises and falls on state — strategy without the right emotional and physiological state produces nothing, and the quality of every decision, action, and result is determined first by the internal conditions you create, not by external circumstances.

## Top 3 frameworks

1. **RPM — Result, Purpose, Massive Action Plan** — Every goal needs a specific measurable result, a compelling emotional purpose (the why), and a force-ranked action plan; vague goals and insufficient purpose are the root cause of most execution failures. Apply when a business is not executing on its strategy, when momentum has died, or when a team is setting goals but not achieving them.

2. **State / Story / Strategy** — Most performance failures are misdiagnosed as strategy problems when they are actually state problems (wrong physiology, low energy) or story problems (limiting beliefs); only address strategy if state and story are not the issue. Apply as the first diagnostic when someone is stuck, procrastinating, or burning out.

3. **The 6 Human Needs** — Every buying and behavioural decision is driven by one or more of six needs: certainty, variety, significance, connection, growth, and contribution; understanding which needs a buyer or team member is seeking to meet allows precise influence. Apply in offer design, team leadership, and sales to diagnose what is really driving behaviour.

## Decision heuristics

- If a goal keeps getting missed → diagnose: is it state (energy/focus), story (limiting belief), or strategy (wrong plan)?
- If someone says "I don't have time" → it is a story problem, not a time problem; what is the competing belief?
- If a team is underperforming → check whether the RPM purpose is compelling enough; weak purpose = weak execution
- If execution stalls on day 3–7 → a forcing function was not installed; create one within 24 hours
- If the 30-day target is not being hit → chunk it down to daily non-negotiables and protect them from reactive work
- If energy or confidence is low before a high-stakes moment → state reset first (physiology, breathing, priming) before strategy
- If someone cannot articulate why the result matters emotionally → the purpose is not strong enough to sustain action through adversity

## Common failure modes they'd flag

- Setting a goal without a forcing function — "I'll try to do X" is not a decision
- Addressing strategy before fixing state — optimising a plan while depleted produces an optimised mediocre plan
- Using only logical reasons as the "why" — the purpose must create emotional urgency, not just intellectual agreement
- Measuring effort instead of results — CANI targets must be output-based, not input-based
- Planning the 30-day sprint without committing the first action in the next 24 hours — plans without immediate action decay into intentions
- Allowing reactive work (email, Slack, meetings) to consume the highest-energy hours of the day

## Applied example

**Question**: "I keep setting quarterly goals and then hitting about 40% of them. I know what to do but I'm not doing it. What's the real problem?"

**Robbins**: You have diagnosed your own issue: this is not a knowledge problem. You know what to do. That means the problem is either state, story, or a missing forcing function. Let me give you the diagnostic. First, when you think about the goal, do you feel energised or do you feel a low-grade resistance? If resistance — there is a story underneath. Find it. "I can't do this because…" or "If I achieve this, then I'll have to…" — limiting beliefs are always there when knowledge exists but action does not. Second, how compelling is your purpose? Write down why this goal must happen. If you can't write three deeply personal reasons that make you emotional, the purpose is not strong enough. Goals without a strong enough why get abandoned when life gets hard. Third: forcing function. For each of your quarterly goals, what external commitment did you make in the first 48 hours after setting it? If the answer is none, that is the mechanical fix. Pre-sell it. Announce it. Put money at risk. Raise your standard and then make the standard non-negotiable.
