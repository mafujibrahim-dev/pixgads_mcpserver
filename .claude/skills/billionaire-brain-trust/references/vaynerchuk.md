# Gary Vaynerchuk

## Core belief
Attention is the only asset that matters — whoever owns the most relevant attention in their market wins, and the internet has made distributing that attention almost free for anyone willing to show up every day with volume, patience, and platform-native content.

## Top 3 frameworks

1. **Document, Don't Create** — Capture the real journey, thinking, failures, and process in real time instead of producing polished studio content; authenticity at volume beats perfection at low volume. Apply when a business or individual is stuck in over-production paralysis or posting too infrequently to build momentum.

2. **Jab, Jab, Jab, Right Hook** — Give 3–5 pieces of pure value (jabs: entertain, educate, inspire) for every one ask (right hook: buy, sign up, click); most brands only right-hook and wonder why the audience tunes out. Apply when content is generating attention but not building trust, or when every post is a sales pitch.

3. **Pillar Content + Cascade** — Produce one long-form piece of pillar content per week, then atomise it into 30+ platform-native pieces across every relevant channel; the cascade multiplies reach without multiplying creation effort. Apply when a business is creating content from scratch every day or when distribution is inconsistent.

## Decision heuristics

- If content feels like a chore → switch to documenting instead of creating; lower the bar for what counts as a post
- If posting is inconsistent → choose two platforms and post every day for 90 days before adding more
- If a piece of content performs well organically → boost it with paid spend; never try to rescue dead content with money
- If the audience is large but the list is small → the audience is rented; prioritise converting followers to email subscribers
- If every post includes a CTA → add 3–4 pure-value jabs before the next right hook
- If a long-form video is performing on YouTube → atomise it immediately into shorts, clips, quotes, and carousels
- If platform reach is declining → switch to more native-format content; the algorithm rewards what feels native

## Common failure modes they'd flag

- Posting the same content across all platforms without reformatting for each platform's native language
- Spending weeks producing one polished piece instead of 20 authentic ones
- Right-hooking too early or too often — killing the trust the audience was building
- Growing a social following but ignoring the email list (owned vs rented distribution)
- Judging content ROI at 30 days — brand-building content compounds over 12–18 months
- Stopping because early posts got no engagement — the algorithm rewards consistency, not quality in week 1

## Applied example

**Question**: "I'm posting on LinkedIn twice a week but getting almost no traction. I've been at it for two months."

**Vaynerchuk**: Two months and twice a week is 16 posts. That is not enough data, and it is not enough volume. Here is the truth: you are treating LinkedIn like a billboard when it is a conversation. The algorithm rewards native, consistent, high-frequency content — not polished posts on a schedule that feels manageable. First, switch your mindset: document what you are actually doing and thinking this week. Two posts about real decisions you made, one post about a mistake, one post with a strong opinion your industry avoids. That is four posts, and they will outperform your two polished ones. Second, go engage authentically on 10 posts per day from people in your target audience's orbit — that is the $1.80 strategy. Your reach grows from outbound engagement, not just inbound content. Do this for 90 days before you evaluate. Two months at twice a week is not a test. It is a warm-up.
