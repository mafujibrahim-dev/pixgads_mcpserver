# Route: positioning — Godin + Kennedy Stack

**Trigger keywords**: niche, category, differentiation, brand, messaging, crowded market, standing out, who am I for, competitive positioning, rebrand, pick a niche

**References**: `references/godin.md`, `references/kennedy.md`

---

## Output: Positioning One-Pager

When this route is active, build a positioning document for the user's specific market, offer, and situation. Fill in with their specifics — do not leave generic templates in the output.

---

### 1. Smallest Viable Market Definition (Godin)

Stop trying to sell to everyone. Define the smallest group that:
- Has the problem acutely (not mildly)
- Shares a worldview (what do they already believe that makes your solution obvious?)
- Has the budget and authority to buy
- Will tell their peers if they love it

**Output format**:
> "[Product] is for [specific person] who [specific belief or situation] and [specific desired transformation] — and no one else."

The exclusion is as important as the inclusion. Naming who it is NOT for sharpens the positioning and elevates perceived exclusivity (Kennedy principle).

---

### 2. Purple Cow Differentiator (Godin)

The one remarkable thing — the single attribute that makes someone stop and say "what's that?"

**Discovery process**:
1. List every claim competitors make (fast, affordable, expert, proven, trusted — these are all beige)
2. Find the edges: What would be too bold? Too specific? Too unusual? Too opinionated?
3. The purple cow lives at the edges
4. Pick one edge and go fully to it — do not sand it down

**Output**:
> "The one remarkable thing about [offer/brand]: [specific, unconventional differentiator]"

Test: Would a prospect remember this in 48 hours and repeat it to someone else? If not, it is not purple enough.

---

### 3. Kennedy "Who Else / Who Not" Exclusion Statement

Every strong position repels the wrong buyer as clearly as it attracts the right one.

**Who it IS for**:
> "[Offer] is specifically for [specific buyer] who [situation], [belief], and [commitment level]."

**Who it is NOT for**:
> "This is NOT for [wrong buyer type], [people who expect X without doing Y], or [people looking for Z]."

The exclusion statement does three things:
- Filters out bad-fit buyers who drain resources
- Makes the right buyer feel seen and chosen
- Signals confidence in the product (you only exclude if you do not need everyone)

---

### 4. Category Claim + Proof Stack

**Category claim** (how you want to be remembered):
> "[Name/Brand] is [the/a] [category descriptor] for [specific audience]."

Examples of strong category claims:
- "The only [X] that [specific mechanism]"
- "The [fastest/most specific/most [adjective]] [X] for [Y]"
- "The [category] that [outcome others can't claim]"

**Proof stack** (3 levels, Kennedy requirement):
1. **Social proof**: Specific testimonials with specific results (numbers, timeframes)
2. **Authority proof**: Media, credentials, case studies, publications
3. **Demonstration proof**: Before/after, track record, proprietary process or data

Do not list credentials — deploy them as answers to the internal objection "why should I believe you?"

---

### 5. Message-to-Market Match Line (Kennedy — headline-ready)

A single sentence that uses the buyer's exact internal language. This is the master headline for the positioning:

**Formula options**:
- "How [specific person] [achieves specific result] without [feared sacrifice]"
- "Finally: [specific result] for [specific person who has tried and failed before]"
- "Warning: [consequence of current approach] — here is what to do instead"
- "[Specific result] in [timeframe]. Guaranteed."

**Test**: Read this line to your ideal buyer. Do they say "that's exactly me" or "interesting"? You need "that's exactly me."
